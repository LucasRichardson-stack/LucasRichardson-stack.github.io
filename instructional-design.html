§ 02Methodology

LAFF: four principles for adult training

A design lens specific to adult learners, who arrive with experience, need relevance, and learn by doing rather than being told.

L

Learner-centric

Start from the learner's needs, background, and experience; design and react to the actual classroom in front of you.

A

Applicable activities

Hands-on, experiential practice tied directly to real job tasks, so knowledge transfers to the workflow.

F

Facilitation

Facilitate, don't lecture: encourage discussion, collaboration, and active problem-solving.

F

Flow

Structure the training in a logical sequence where each lesson builds on the last.

LAFF complements established models rather than replacing them: ADDIE for process, Backward Design for starting from outcomes, Bloom's Taxonomy for pitching objectives at the right cognitive level, and Rapid Prototyping for iterating quickly. Underneath all of them sit the seven andragogical assumptions of adult learning: a need to know, self-direction, drawing on experience, readiness, problem-orientation, internal motivation, and relevance.

§ 04AI in the Workflow

Where AI cuts development time

AI does not replace instructional-design judgment; it compresses the mechanical first drafts so the designer spends limited time on the parts that need expertise. Savings concentrate in three early-stage tasks.

Task 01

Workflow mapping

Feed AI a use case and a reference document set; get a first-pass breakdown of stages and tasks to structure the course. The designer edits and validates rather than starting from a blank page.

Drafting: hours → minutes
Task 02

Storyboarding

Generate draft slide-by-slide storyboards, speaker-note scaffolds, and timing estimates from an approved outline. Storyboarding is roughly 11% of build time; a strong first draft removes much of it.

First-draft storyboard in one pass
Task 03

Learning objectives

Turn a task list into draft action-oriented objectives at the right Bloom's level, then refine for observability. AI is fast at the verbs; the designer owns alignment to performance.

Draft objectives per task, instantly

NET EFFECT: a conservative 25–30% reduction in development hours, concentrated in analysis and design. Production and review still take human time, and the design decisions, what to teach, in what order, assessed how, remain the designer's. AI accelerates the draft; it does not own the judgment.

§ 05Module Design

The build pattern

Every hands-on module follows the same arc, so learners always know where they are and each lesson reinforces the workflow.

01

Short intro

Explain why this module matters in the context of the real use case.

02

Relevant theory

Technical explanation with diagrams and renderings, limited to what the task uses.

03

Live demo

Demonstrate exactly the task the learner is about to perform.

04

Hands-on task

A relevant, challenging task that puts the theory into practice.

05

Wrap-up

Reflect, discuss, and address questions to consolidate the learning.

Concept Check / Try it

Write a performance objective

A module teaches a technician to set up a vision system's coordinate frame. Which objective is written correctly? Reveal each to see why.

Weak"Understand how the coordinate frame works."▶
"Understand" is not observable; you cannot watch someone understand, and you cannot assess it directly. Knowledge verbs hide whether the learner can actually do the task.Rewrite as a demonstrable action.
Strong"Configure the coordinate frame so the robot reaches all part positions with clearance."▶
Action-oriented ("Configure"), observable, and tied to a real performance standard (reach + clearance). A trainer can watch it happen and assess it against a clear criterion.A verb you can see, a standard you can measure.
Knowledge Check

Pick the design that fits the principle

You are teaching a hands-on robotics setup task. Applying the "applicable activities" principle, what should the module's core activity be?

§ 06Structuring Content

Map the workflow, then teach it

Training mirrors the real or ideal use case. The workflow becomes the course; its stages become modules; its tasks become lessons with objectives.

Step 01

Identify the use case

Define a common, relevant scenario containing the key skills.

e.g. "Install a vision system"
Step 02

Break into stages

Map the use case into its main stages; these form the course.

e.g. "Do a reach study"
Step 03

Identify tasks

Break each stage into tasks; these become modules or lessons.

e.g. "Confirm part is in line of sight"
Step 04

Write objectives

Write an action-oriented, observable objective per task.

e.g. "Mount to reach all parts w/ clearance"
§ 07Evaluation

Measure performance, then improve

Evaluation closes the loop: assess against the performance goals, gather feedback, and iterate on the next version.

During

Formative assessment

Ongoing checks throughout the training measure progress toward goals and let the facilitator adjust content and delivery in real time.

After

Summative assessment

End-of-training assessment evaluates whether performance goals were met and how effective the program was overall.

Qualitative

Gathering feedback

Collect participant input on relevance, engagement, and value to surface what to improve.

Loop

Continuous improvement

Analyze results and feedback to iterate on content, delivery, and design for future cohorts.

§ 08Quality Gate

Module design checklist

Before a module ships, it should clear every item below. Tap to check them off; this is the working QA list, not decoration.

Real-life use case identified; training reflects actual job tasks.
Workflow broken into clear stages.
Tasks defined within each stage.
Action-oriented, observable performance goals written per task.
Each module has intro, theory, demo, hands-on task, and wrap-up.
Theory is relevant, visualized, and limited to what the task uses.
Speaker notes and timing estimates on each slide.
Demo aligns exactly with the hands-on task.
Hands-on task is realistic, challenging, and aligned to the objective.
Links to relevant documentation for each task.
Wrap-up includes reflection and discussion.
Success criteria defined so the task is assessable.
